It forecasts where the attack goes next.

Most intrusion tooling answers “is this bad?” SENTINEL answers “what happens next, how soon, and on what evidence?” — a K-step open-loop forecast across seven MITRE stages, scored against real CIC-IDS2017 traffic rather than only synthetic replay.

What it actually does

Four properties that decide whether a forecast is worth an analyst’s attention.

K-step open-loop rollout

Given a window of flow features, the world model rolls forward several steps with no further input, so the forecast is a claim about the future — not a smoothing of the present.

Observed and forecast never blend

Confirmed attack evidence sits outside the risk colour ramp on purpose. You can always tell what the network did from what the model expects.

A deployment baseline, not a global mean

Detectors score against a median/MAD baseline fitted on disjoint benign reference traffic, so “unusual” is relative to this network rather than to a constant every network shares.

Generalisation is measured, not assumed

Leave-one-entity-out over real windows, with per-stage breakdown, so an unseen-stage number cannot hide behind a pooled average.

Read the 0.242 before the 0.892.

On real traffic this model is evidence for a human analyst, not a blocking control. A 0.242 F1 and a 16.1% lateral-movement alert rate mean the network still catches most attacks by hand, and the synthetic figure is both the larger number and the one that generalises worse. The deployment baseline exists because the raw detector flagged 99.2% of benign traffic; it is mitigation, not resolution.
